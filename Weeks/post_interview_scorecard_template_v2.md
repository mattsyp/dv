# Post-Interview Scorecard Feedback Template

Use this template after an interview to turn raw interview notes and
interviewer impressions into evidence-based scorecard feedback.

## How to Use This Template

Provide the following in the chat:

1.  The questions asked and the candidate's responses/notes.
2.  Your own impressions from the interview, including strengths,
    concerns, and your tentative hiring recommendation.
3.  The company's values/rubric if they are not already available in the
    conversation or project.

When creating the feedback:

-   Treat the interview notes as **evidence** and the interviewer's
    impressions as **assessment/context**.
-   Do not invent evidence or assume the candidate lacks a skill simply
    because it did not appear in the interview.
-   Prefer language such as **"I did not get enough evidence of..."**
    over **"The candidate cannot..."** when the interview did not
    establish the capability.
-   Evaluate the candidate against the level and needs of the role being
    filled, not their current title.
-   Include meaningful positive evidence even when the final
    recommendation is No Hire.
-   Technical questions do not need to be treated as value questions
    during the interview. When writing the scorecard, incorporate
    relevant technical evidence into the Core Value it most strongly
    supports. Make clear in the assessment when the evidence is
    primarily a role-specific technical signal.
-   All interview evidence must fit within the five Core Value sections
    and the final Key Take-Aways. Do not create additional technical,
    role-specific, or competency sections.
-   Avoid duplicating the same evidence across multiple values unless it
    genuinely provides distinct signals for each value.
-   Keep the final Key Take-Aways to **no more than two paragraphs**. Do
    not repeat all of the detailed evidence already captured under the
    values.

# Scorecard Evidence

The submitted scorecard has exactly five Core Value sections plus Key
Take-Aways. Use one section for each company value. All behavioral,
technical, operational, and role-specific evidence must be incorporated
into the value it most strongly supports.

Technical evidence can still be identified as technical or role-specific
within the **Assessment**. Do not create separate sections for technical
depth, observability, architecture, event-driven systems, or other
competencies.

# Key Take-Aways

**Maximum: two paragraphs.**

Paragraph 1 should briefly acknowledge the strongest positive evidence
and summarize the candidate's overall strengths.

Paragraph 2 should identify the small number of concerns that drive the
hiring decision, connect them directly to the needs/bar of the role, and
state the recommendation clearly.

Example structure:

> \[Candidate\] demonstrated positive signals around \[2-3 strongest
> areas\]. \[Brief statement of what the evidence showed\]. However,
> across the interview I did not get enough evidence of \[key
> capabilities required for this role\].
>
> I am looking for a \[role level\] who can \[most important
> expectations for this particular team\]. The interview did not provide
> sufficient evidence of \[2-4 decisive gaps/signals\]. Based on the
> overall evidence and the bar for this role, my recommendation is
> \[Hire / No Hire\].

# Quality Check Before Submitting

Before finalizing the scorecard, check that:

-   Every significant conclusion can be traced back to something the
    candidate actually said or did in the interview.
-   Missing evidence is described as missing/insufficient evidence
    rather than proof that the candidate lacks the capability.
-   Positive evidence is preserved even when the recommendation is No
    Hire.
-   Concerns explain **why they matter for this specific role**, not
    merely why an answer was imperfect.
-   The assessment evaluates the candidate against the role being
    filled, not against their current title.
-   The same anecdote is not over-counted as strong evidence across
    multiple values without explaining why it applies.
-   Technical depth, collaboration, ownership, mentorship, ambiguity,
    adversity, and operational maturity are considered when relevant to
    the role and incorporated into the most relevant Core Value section.
-   The final output contains only the five Core Value sections and Key
    Take-Aways; no additional scorecard sections are added.
-   The Key Take-Aways are no more than two paragraphs and do not
    duplicate the detailed scorecard notes.
-   The final recommendation follows from the evidence presented above
    it.
