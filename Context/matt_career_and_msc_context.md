## 4. Leadership Style

Matt’s leadership style is direct, practical and systems-oriented.

He prefers to start by understanding the real operating environment:

- 1:1s with engineers, especially senior and staff engineers
- Conversations with PMs and cross-functional partners
- Review of incidents, support patterns and workflow friction
- Comparison between official process and actual process
- Identification of keystone blockers that create repeated pain

He does not default to adding process for its own sake. He looks for the smallest operating system that gives a team better clarity, better ownership and better execution.

Common leadership beliefs:

- Strong teams need clarity more than ceremony
- Managers should not become routing layers for every decision
- Senior ICs and Staff engineers should create leverage, not dependency
- Product and Engineering need shared tradeoff visibility
- Technical debt and leadership debt often reinforce each other
- Support load, incidents and unclear ownership are usually symptoms of deeper system problems
- AI can accelerate thinking and delivery, but judgment and accountability stay human

A useful phrase for Matt’s leadership style:

> High performance without heroics.

## 8. Leadership Debt Framework

Leadership Debt is one of Matt’s core concepts.

Leadership debt is the hidden cost created when leadership systems do not scale with the team. It shows up when managers, senior ICs or executives compensate for unclear ownership, weak decision-making, missing operating rhythms or poor accountability.

Common signs of leadership debt:

- Too many decisions flow through one person
- Managers are busy but not creating leverage
- Senior engineers become permanent bottlenecks
- Teams rely on escalation instead of ownership
- Work moves through heroics instead of systems
- Product and Engineering tradeoffs are implicit instead of explicit
- People confuse activity with execution
- Onboarding depends on tribal knowledge
- Repeated support issues are treated as isolated tickets
- Planning looks clean but reality is chaotic

Matt’s approach to leadership debt is practical. The goal is not to write a theory paper. The goal is to identify the debt, make it visible and build systems that reduce it.
